Understanding air quality

This page uses the Indian National Air Quality Index from the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB). It is not the US EPA AQI: the breakpoints and category names differ, so the same number can mean different things.

What AQI means

The AQI combines measurements of several pollutants into one number from 0 to 500. A higher number means more polluted air. It helps people decide when to take extra care, such as limiting long outdoor exercise on high-pollution days.

PM2.5 and PM10

PM2.5 is particulate matter 2.5 micrometres or smaller, small enough to reach deep into the lungs. PM10 is 10 micrometres or smaller and includes road and construction dust.

Concentration is not AQI

Concentration is a physical measurement in µg/m³. AQI is a calculated score: each pollutant becomes a sub-index and the overall AQI is the highest one. A PM2.5 of 60 µg/m³ is not an AQI of 60.

Why weather matters

Wind carries pollutants away while calm air lets them build up. Rain washes particles out. Cool, still nights trap pollution near the ground, and heat and sunshine can help form ozone.

Is AQI the same as PM2.5?

No. PM2.5 is a pollutant measured as a concentration. AQI is a score calculated from the concentrations of several pollutants.

Can two websites show different AQI for the same city?

Yes. They may use different monitoring stations, different times, or different AQI standards, such as CPCB versus US EPA.

Does a low AQI mean the air is perfectly safe?

Not necessarily. AQI is a summary at one place and time. Sensitive people may still want to take care, and readings change through the day.
